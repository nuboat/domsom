# Literature Note: Batson, Kobrynowicz, Dinnerstein, Kampf, & Wilson (1997)

- **APA 7 Reference:** Batson, C. D., Kobrynowicz, D., Dinnerstein, J. L., Kampf, H. C., & Wilson, A. D. (1997). In a very different voice: Unmasking moral hypocrisy. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 72(6), 1335–1348. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.72.6.1335
- **Journal & Quartile:** *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* | SJR 2024: 3.85 | Best Quartile: Q1 (Social Psychology) | Checked: 2026-10-29.
- **Evidence Level:** `[FT]` Full Text Verified.
- **Purpose & RQ:** What is the primary motivation underlying moral behavior: genuine moral integrity (acting morally even at a cost) or moral hypocrisy (appearing moral while avoiding the cost)?
- **Theoretical Lens:** Theory of Moral Hypocrisy, Social Desirability, Self-Deception.
- **Design & Methodology:** Seminal experimental behavioral paradigm (N = 80). Participants allocated two tasks (one rewarding with raffle tickets, one tedious and unrewarded) between themselves and an anonymous other participant, with or without a private coin-toss randomization device.
- **Constructs & Measures:** Moral Integrity vs. Moral Hypocrisy; Task Assignment Choice; Perceived Fairness Rating; Coin Flip Utilization Rate.
- **Key Findings & Effect Sizes:**
  - Even when given a fair mechanism (coin flip) in private, over 80% of participants who flipped the coin assigned the positive task to themselves ($p < .001$), while rating their decision as completely fair and moral.
  - Demonstrates that human moral posturing is dominated by moral hypocrisy: the drive to enjoy the social and self-concept rewards of morality without paying the cost.
- **Claimed Contribution:** The pioneering empirical demonstration and formal operationalization of moral hypocrisy in social psychology.
- **Author-Stated Limitations:** Lab setting with student sample; binary allocation task.
- **Critique & Boundary Conditions:** Conducted in physical lab isolation; modern digital environments introduce anonymous disinhibition and amplified audience signaling.
- **Relevance to This Study:** The foundational theoretical anchor for Chapter 1 and the conceptual core of "The Hypocrisy of Parity".
