\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Macro Level: The Rise of Egalitarian Ideals and Rationalized Discourse in Interpersonal Life}
Over the past two decades, cultural norms governing interpersonal relationships, domestic cohabitation, and the treatment of others have undergone a profound dual transformation \citep{swales1990genre,grossmann2023ai}. First, traditional authoritarian models have been widely superseded by ideals of absolute egalitarianism, mutual emotional validation, and strict parity in labor, respect, and mutual accommodation. Principles such as ``equal partnership,'' ``fair share,'' ``emotional labor,'' and ``structural equity'' have become foundational benchmarks of relational ethics. Second, contemporary interpersonal culture has internalized a powerful norm of communicative rationalization: individuals are expected to articulate their needs through reasoned dialogue, emotional literacy, and psychological terminology (``holding space,'' ``protecting boundaries,'' ``processing burnout''). In theory, this intersection of egalitarian values and reasoned communication represents an enlightened ethical evolution, providing partners with shared discursive tools to resolve friction fairly and prevent exploitation.

However, foundational scholarship in social psychology establishes that high-minded ethical declarations rarely guarantee moral conduct. As \citet{batson1997unmasking,batson1999moral} demonstrated in classic experimental paradigms, human moral behavior is frequently characterized by \textit{moral hypocrisy}: the pervasive motivation to appear fair, ethical, and principled to oneself and others while covertly evading the behavioral and material sacrifices required by genuine fairness. While external surveillance and public monitoring can constrain overt hypocrisy in formal institutional settings, private interpersonal interactions operate under fundamentally distinct conditions. Behind closed doors—across romantic couples, domestic cohabitants, and close peer ties—power dynamics are emotionally charged, asymmetric, and shielded from third-party observation. Under these conditions, the dual vocabularies of equality and reasoned explanation are readily co-opted into potent interpersonal levers, detached from actual conduct \citep{cerulli2026dark}.

\subsection{Meso Level: Weaponized Egalitarian Posturing (WEP) and ``I Have My Reasons'' as Discursive Instruments of Exploitation}
To advance beyond simplistic critiques of egalitarianism, relationship science must distinguish between sincere advocacy for mutual fairness and what we conceptualize as \textbf{Weaponized Egalitarian Posturing (WEP)}. Sincere egalitarian partners deploy fairness standards to build reciprocal, cooperative arrangements. In stark contrast, individuals engaging in WEP instrumentalize parity terminology as an offensive leverage device—rigidly policing chores, emotional attentiveness, and reciprocal sacrifices under the banner of fairness.

Yet, an acute behavioral breakdown unfolds whenever the posturing actor fails to reciprocate. When called upon to execute domestic tasks, provide emotional care, or share financial burdens, actors engaging in WEP systematically mobilize intellectualized, plausible justifications—encapsulated by the ubiquitous conversational stance: \textbf{``I Have My Reasons'' (or Thai ``	extit{I Have My Reasons''})} \citep{scott1968accounts,edwards1992discursive}. These rationalized accounts appeal to situational constraints, career exhaustion, mental health boundaries, or unique stress loads (e.g., ``Given how intense my workload has been, it is only logical that you handle the domestic logistics''; ``I fully believe in 50/50, but I have valid personal reasons why I cannot contribute right now'').

By pairing uncompromising demands for parity from the other person with bulletproof, rationalized exemptions for oneself, actors construct an unassailable moral fortress. This discursive maneuver converts prior egalitarian posturing into ``moral credentials'' \citep{monin2001moral,merritt2010moral}, allowing individuals to engage in ongoing, one-sided exploitation (unilateral labor offloading, financial parasitism, emotional withdrawal) completely insulated from internal guilt and shielded against external reproach \citep{bandura1999moral,bates2024virtuous}.

\subsection{Micro Level: Two-Study Cross-Cultural Computational Architecture \& Research Questions}
Investigating this subtle, rationalized hypocrisy poses a severe methodological challenge. Traditional laboratory experiments lack mundane realism, while self-report surveys suffer from near-fatal \textit{social desirability bias}. Perpetrators of interpersonal exploitation almost never confess on Likert scales to weaponizing equality rhetoric or using plausible accounts to exploit their partner. 

To overcome these vulnerabilities, this study introduces a **Two-Study Cross-Cultural Computational Mixed-Methods Design** that analyzes authentic first-person moral-judgment seeking disclosures:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Study 1 (English, Individualistic Context):} Analyzes $N = 10,000$ archival conflict narratives from Reddit's \textit{r/AmItheAsshole}, where narrators explicitly articulate their relationship philosophy and defend their conduct before a community of peers.
    \item \textbf{Study 2 (Thai, Collectivist Context):} Replicates the empirical model across $N = 4,250$ systematically filtered relationship dispute disclosures from Pantip, isolating moral-judgment seeking narratives through structural keyword queries (e.g., ``Am I wrong for...'', ``Am I selfish if...'') paired with equality terms (``50/50'', ``split evenly'', ``fair'').
\end{enumerate}

Furthermore, to eliminate single-source algorithmic bias, we implement a **Split-Source Validation Architecture**: narrator internal discursive constructs ($WEP$, $LRA$, $SSA$) are extracted from narrative text using standardized bilingual Large Language Models (decoding Temperature = 0.10), while behavioral exploitation ($EIR$) is triangulated with **Crowd-Sourced Moral Verdicts** (e.g., Reddit crowd verdict `YTA` vs `NTA` and communal accountability voting).

Specifically, this study addresses three FINER research questions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Weaponized Posturing vs. Relational Extraction):} To what extent does Weaponized Egalitarian Posturing (\textit{WEP}) predict Exploitative Interpersonal Relations (\textit{EIR}) across real-world relationship narratives, and how does Self-Serving Attribution (\textit{SSA}) mediate this dynamic?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Rationalized Accounts as an Interpersonal Catalyst):} How do Legitimized Rational Accounts (\textit{LRA} — ``I Have My Reasons'' / ``	extit{I Have My Reasons''}) moderate the link between parity rhetoric and self-serving justifications, accelerating moral licensing and neutralizing relational guilt?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Cross-Cultural Invariance \& Crowd Triangulation):} How consistently does the moderated mediation architecture replicate across individualistic (English Reddit) and collectivist (Thai Pantip) conflict disclosures when validated against crowd-adjudicated moral verdicts?
\end{itemize}
