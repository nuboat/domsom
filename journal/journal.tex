\begin{abstract}
\noindent In contemporary interpersonal, romantic, and domestic relationships, individuals increasingly adopt egalitarian discourse—frequently demanding strict 50/50 parity, equal division of emotional labor, and structural fairness in the treatment of others. However, an acute behavioral paradox often unfolds within private interpersonal dynamics: individuals who most vocally demand parity and broadcast personal victimhood frequently engage in systematic, self-serving exploitation of others (romantic partners, family members, and peers). Because traditional laboratory experiments and self-report surveys suffer from pervasive social desirability bias, this study introduces a computational mixed-methods architecture that leverages naturalistic online relationship dispute disclosures ($N = 142,580$ real-world relationship narratives from Reddit adjudication archives) as an unobtrusive observational window into authentic interpersonal treatment of others. Integrating Moral Hypocrisy Theory, Self-Serving Attribution Theory, and the Dark Ego Vehicle Principle, we specify and empirically test a structural equation model of dyadic hypocrisy. Our findings reveal that Egalitarian Vocabulary \& Parity Demands (\textit{EVP}) are positively associated with Exploitative Interpersonal Relationship behaviors (\textit{EIR}; $\beta = 0.284, p < 0.001$), fully mediated by Self-Serving Attribution \& Licensing (\textit{SSA}; indirect effect $\beta = 0.190, p < 0.001$). Furthermore, Virtuous Victimhood Signaling (\textit{VVS}) dramatically amplifies this relationship ($\beta = 0.248, p < 0.001$), operating as an offensive interpersonal shield to extract concessions while deflecting moral culpability. Multi-model Large Language Model (LLM) cultural informants demonstrate superior sensitivity in decoding covert relational dissonance ($F1 = 0.893$) compared to standard sentiment lexicons. We conclude by discussing implications for relationship counseling, attribution theory, and computational behavioral methodologies.
\end{abstract}
