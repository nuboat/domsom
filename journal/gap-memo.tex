\begin{abstract}
This memorandum establishes the formal scope, theoretical problematization, and research gap classification for the manuscript \textit{"Weaponized Egalitarian Posturing: Discursive Rationalization and Exploitative Interpersonal Relations across Western and Thai Conflict Narratives"}. Grounded in Swales' CARS framework and Alvesson \& Sandberg's (2011) problematization methodology, this document maps four intersecting scholarly conversations, classifies the core theoretical paradox within real-world interpersonal relationships, articulates three FINER research questions, and conducts a pre-mortem audit to eliminate fatal methodological vulnerabilities prior to full empirical deployment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scholarly Conversation Mapping}
This study intervenes at the nexus of four intersecting scholarly conversations spanning Discursive Psychology, Interpersonal Communication, Social Psychology, and Computational Social Science:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conversation 1: Moral Hypocrisy \& Self-Serving Attribution in Relationships.} Foundational works establish that human moral action is frequently guided by the desire to appear fair and principled without incurring the behavioral costs of sacrifice \citep{batson1997unmasking,batson1999moral}. In close relationships, this is reinforced by self-serving attribution and moral licensing, wherein claiming egalitarian values creates cognitive credentials that insulate actors from guilt during subsequent selfish conduct \citep{monin2001moral,merritt2010moral,krettenauer2022when}.
    \item \textbf{Conversation 2: Discursive Psychology, Blame Management, \& Interpersonal Accounts.} Research in discursive psychology and interpersonal communication demonstrates that language in dispute situations functions as a performative social action \citep{edwards1992discursive,billig1996arguing}. Actors systematically deploy conversational accounts, justifications, and excuses \citep{scott1968accounts} to manage accountability, discursively construct plausible explanations (``I Have My Reasons''), and deflect responsibility for relationship friction.
    \item \textbf{Conversation 3: Interpersonal Exploitation \& Instrumental Rationalization.} Personality and social psychology demonstrate that entitled and Machiavellian actors deploy moral grandstanding, instrumental rationalization, and discursive excuses to secure relational control, emotional compliance, and unearned partner accommodations \citep{bandura1999moral,savejnarong2022interpersonal,cerulli2026dark}.
    \item \textbf{Conversation 4: Cross-Cultural Computational Social Science \& Crowd-Consensus Validation.} Advances in NLP establish that LLMs, when calibrated against human experts ($\alpha \ge 0.82$) and triangulated with crowd moral verdicts, can reliably capture subtle conversational subtexts and decode latent hypocrisy across massive naturalistic dispute corpora, bypassing self-report social desirability bias \citep{ziems2024large,argyle2023out,grossmann2024generative}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problematization: Beyond Gap-Spotting}
Traditional research on relationship equity and domestic labor relies on simple \textit{gap-spotting} (e.g., investigating whether gender roles are more equal in a specific demographic). Following \citet{alvesson2011generating}, this study pursues \textbf{problematization} by challenging the foundational assumption shared by contemporary relationship science:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Conventional Assumption:} Vocal demands for 50/50 equality, emotional labor sharing, and relationship fairness reflect prosocial dyadic commitment aimed at reducing relational friction.
\end{quote}
\textbf{Problematized Axiom (First Principles):} In interpersonal relationships, egalitarian vocabulary is structurally vulnerable to being co-opted as \textbf{Weaponized Egalitarian Posturing (WEP)}. Far from promoting equity, high-frequency parity demands combined with plausible personal excuses (``I Have My Reasons'') actively lower the perceived moral cost of exploiting others by establishing moral credentials and pre-empting accountability.

\section{FINER Research Questions}
Applying the FINER criteria (\textit{Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant}) and safeguarding against Type III errors \citep{alvesson2011generating}, the inquiry is anchored by three core questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Weaponized Posturing vs. Relational Extraction):} To what extent does Weaponized Egalitarian Posturing (\textit{WEP}) predict Exploitative Interpersonal Relations (\textit{EIR}) across real-world relationship narratives, and how does Self-Serving Attribution (\textit{SSA}) mediate this dynamic?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Rationalized Accounts as an Interpersonal Catalyst):} How do Legitimized Rational Accounts (\textit{LRA} --- ``I Have My Reasons'') moderate the link between parity rhetoric and self-serving justifications, accelerating moral licensing and neutralizing guilt?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Cross-Cultural Invariance \& Crowd Triangulation):} How consistently does the moderated mediation architecture replicate across individualistic (English Reddit, $N = 142,580$) and collectivist (Thai Pantip, $N = 4,250$) conflict disclosures when validated against crowd-adjudicated moral verdicts?
\end{itemize}
